About Rotary

Rotary International

A global network of over a million neighbors, friends, and problem-solvers who unite to make lasting change in their communities and the world.

What Is Rotary

People of Action, Worldwide

Rotary International connects more than a million business, professional, and community leaders across some 46,000 clubs in over 200 countries and geographic areas. Founded in 1905 as one of the world's first service organizations, Rotary brings together people from all walks of life who share one motto: Service Above Self.

Rotary's mission is to provide service to others, promote integrity, and advance world understanding, goodwill, and peace through a worldwide fellowship of business, professional, and community leaders. As part of District 5330, San Jacinto Rotary Club carries that same mission into the San Jacinto Valley every week.

The Object of Rotary

Why Rotary Exists

Rotary's founding purpose is to encourage and foster the ideal of service as the basis of worthy enterprise. In practice, that means:

  • Building genuine friendships as an opportunity for service
  • Holding high ethical standards in business and every profession, and recognizing the value of all useful work
  • Applying the ideal of service in each member's personal, business, and community life
  • Advancing international understanding, goodwill, and peace through a worldwide fellowship united in service
Guiding Principles

The Avenues of Service

Every Rotary club, including ours, channels its work through five Avenues of Service — the philosophical foundation for everything a club does.

Club Service

Strengthening fellowship among members and keeping the club itself running well, so it can keep serving others.

Vocational Service

Encouraging members to serve through their own trades and professions, and to act with integrity in their work.

Community Service

The hands-on projects — like our Peach of a Deal fundraiser and scholarships — that improve life close to home.

International Service

Extending goodwill and support beyond our own community, in partnership with Rotarians around the world.

New Generations Service

Investing in young people through leadership, mentorship, and exchange programs — from our support of District 5330's PRYDE camp for local 7th graders to the scholarships we award graduating seniors.

Recited at Every Meeting

The Four-Way Test

Of the things we think, say, or do:

  • Is it the TRUTH?
  • Is it FAIR to all concerned?
  • Will it build GOODWILL and better friendships?
  • Will it be BENEFICIAL to all concerned?

Written by Rotarian Herbert J. Taylor in 1932, the Four-Way Test has since been translated into more than 100 languages and remains Rotary's ethical compass for personal and professional life.

Same Principles, Local Impact

How This Shows Up in San Jacinto

These guiding principles aren't just words on a page — they're the standard our club and every club in District 5330, including our friends at Hemet Rotary, hold ourselves to each week. Whether we're delivering peaches and apples, awarding a scholarship, or sponsoring a student for Leadership Camp, the Object of Rotary and the Four-Way Test shape how we do it.
